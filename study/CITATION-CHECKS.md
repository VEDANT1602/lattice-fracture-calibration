# Citation checks for the lattice benchmark

1. Hexner, Liu and Nagel (2018). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.97.063001). [Complete author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.06153). The passage on local modulus and its connection to bond removal, including the relationship between bond-level and global elastic moduli, supports the opening observation. Its full-vector disordered spring networks are a separate model. The 2% tolerances and timing claims come from the accompanying records.
2. Rozza, Huynh and Patera (2008). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11831-008-9019-9). [Complete article PDF](https://www.uni-ulm.de/fileadmin/website_uni_ulm/mawi.inst.070/ss12/NumPDE2/Literatur/Rozza-Huynh-Patera_RBApproxAndAPostErrorEstForAffinelyParameterizedEllipticCoercivePDEs.pdf). Introduction, page 230, discussion of many-query applications and “Opportunity II,” supports separating offline preparation and per-query cost. The paper's reduced-basis benchmarks are distinct from the three solver workloads here.
3. Nachmias (2020). [Open full chapter](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-27968-4_2). Theorem 2.28 gives the minimum-energy unit-flow variational principle. Lemma 2.32 gives the unit-potential principle for effective conductance. In this benchmark the imposed zero-displacement perturbation on the clamped grip nodes amounts to a wired boundary for the two-terminal dipole. Evaluating the old flow/voltage in the heterogeneous medium gives our pair of inequalities.
4. Henderson and Searle (1981). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/1023004). [Full author technical report at Cornell](https://ecommons.cornell.edu/bitstream/1813/32749/1/BU-647-M.pdf), equations for the inverse of an additive modification, including the product-form update on printed page 7, equations (21)–(26) and historical references. Our calculation contracts the general matrix modification on both sides with the dipole. This source supplies the actual derivation consulted. No claim of independently reviewing the 1950 articles is needed.
5. Barrett and coauthors (1994). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/1.9781611971538). [Full text at Netlib](https://netlib.org/templates/templates.pdf), second electronic edition, section 2.3.1 “The Conjugate Gradient Method,” Figure 2.5 and accompanying paragraph on the preconditioner. Supports the PCG recurrence. The certified scalar stopping test is derived in the manuscript.

6. Chen, Davis, Hager and Rajamanickam (2008). [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1391989.1391995) and [author PDF](https://people.clas.ufl.edu/hager/files/cholmod_alg.pdf). The introductory algorithm description identifies sparse positive-definite factorization and triangular solves. The [CVXOPT sparse solver manual](https://cvxopt.org/userguide/spsolvers.html) documents separate symbolic/numeric phases, in-place solution and the supernodal setting. This implementation uses the wheel-bundled native CHOLMOD library.
7. Bell, Olson and Schroder (2022). [Open article](https://joss.theoj.org/papers/10.21105/joss.04142). Documents the PyAMG solver library, including classical AMG. The [Ruge–Stüben API](https://pyamg.readthedocs.io/en/latest/generated/pyamg.classical.html) and the installed 5.4.1 function signature identify the interpolation, strength and relaxation defaults used in this experiment. The observed wall times belong to this benchmark, not either cited software paper.

No previous version of this author’s repository is cited as peer-reviewed prior work.
